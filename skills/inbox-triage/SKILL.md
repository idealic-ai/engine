Read-only triage of an intake inbox signal: gather the detail that makes it ticketable, then report. Investigate and hand back — never edit, file, or post.

# /inbox-triage Protocol (The Triage Investigator)

[!!!] **Sessionless and read-only toward the product.** It **never edits product data and never files tickets**; the disposition stays with the orchestrator (`¶INV_INTAKE_DISPATCHES_NEVER_EXECUTES`, `¶INV_TRIAGE_BEFORE_TICKET`).

[!!!] **Posting is an argument, and the default is `--post`** (`¶INV_TERMINAL_PRODUCER_POSTS`). The single write this skill is permitted is one comment onto the origin thread.

[!!!] **What you post is NOT your report** (`¶INV_COMMENT_REPORTS_THE_SUBJECT_NOT_THE_METHOD`). The report is an investigation record written for the next triage pass; the comment is written for **the person who owns the bug**, from `assets/TEMPLATE_TICKET_COMMENT.md`, and the report rides it as an attachment. Detail is welcome — length was never the problem. *Subject* is: above the one-line footer, every sentence is about the defect, never about how the defect was investigated.

*   **`--post` (default)** — you are the terminal producer of this finding: run standalone, or dispatched singly by the orchestrator. Post it.
*   **`--no-post`** — you are an *intermediate*: one of two angles in a triangulation. **Return the report and post nothing.** Two angle-reports landing on a thread before adjudication show the reporter two possibly-contradictory answers and a third comment resolving them — intermediates presented as conclusions.

**What actually prevents a report going unposted is the gate, not the poster.** One wave produced three complete investigations that were written, never posted, and found days later — because posting was a trailing bullet with nothing checking it. The fix is that whoever posts must **return the resolvable comment URL**, and the caller's accounting cannot complete without it (`triageAccounting`). Moving the write around does not help; making it verifiable does.

> **The origin signal you are given includes the thread's REPLIES, not just the comment body** (`¶INV_REPLIES_ARE_SIGNAL`). Read them. Prior triage results, corrections and the reporter's own follow-ups live there, and they are often the most-processed evidence on the item — a wave once filed a ticket asserting something had never been investigated, four days after a reply on that same thread gave the answer. If your prompt passed you only a body, **say so in your report**: it means your caller withheld material you needed.
>
> **And if the claim you were handed looks wrong, say that instead of answering it.** *"The premise of this question is false, and here is why"* is a more useful result than a careful answer to the wrong question. You are being asked to test a claim, not to assume it.

> **Screenshots and attachments on the signal are evidence — open them.** With Linear, pass the markdown holding the image reference to **`mcp__linear__extract_images`**. **Never `curl` an `uploads.linear.app` URL**: it returns 401 and always will, because the bare `assetUrl` is a reference rather than a fetchable link. Reporting "I could not see the attachment" after a failed `curl` is a **false negative you caused**, not a limit you found. And read the image against the claim — a capture often fails to show the thing the reporter says it shows, which is a finding worth more than agreeing with them.

> **If you are one angle of a triangulation**, a sibling is investigating the same signal from a different vantage point. **You do not need to do anything to stay independent of it** — it posts nothing and writes to a different file, so there is no output of theirs you could see. Read your prompt, investigate your assigned angle, return. Prior-wave `✅ result` replies remain fine to read: both angles start from the same shared context, and symmetric priors do not compromise independence — seeing each other's *conclusions* would.

**Triage ≠ research.** This is *light*: identify, reproduce, relate — enough to make a signal ticketable. Deep root-causing happens later, on a graduated `Needs research` ticket.

## Input
The handoff prompt (scaffold: `/intake`'s `assets/TEMPLATE_HANDOFF_PROMPT.md`) — the origin signal (reporter, what they said, screenshots/attachments), the specific gaps to close, known context, and the project's **Directions**. If invoked with only a comment URL/id, read the comment (and its attachments) first to reconstruct the signal.

**Read all three surfaces, not just the channel's.** An intake project's context is split across: the **Project description** (project-wide — `## Directions`, `## Stakeholders`), each **inbox channel ticket's description** (that channel's one-line scope + its `## Directions`), and the project's **Inbox Handbook** document (`¶INV_CHANNEL_MACHINERY_IN_ONE_DOC` — the shared machinery: report template, what-happens-next, how a comment becomes a ticket, the `## Ticketing Strategy`, plus **this project's own `## What triage will chase`**, which names the tools that actually identify a subject here and is deliberately different in every project — never reuse another project's). When dispatched by a wave, all of it arrives in the handoff prompt — treat it as complete and do **not** go re-read the project. Running standalone with only a comment id, read all three yourself; reading only the channel silently drops both the project-level steer and the recipe.

**Channels are deliberately thin now.** If a channel description looks sparse, that is correct — the machinery was hoisted to the handbook so one edit updates every channel. Do not treat a thin channel as a missing spec, and do not reconstruct the handbook from a channel.

**Directions outrank the recipe** (`¶INV_DIRECTIONS_IN_DESCRIPTIONS`). They say what this project is chasing right now, what counts as enough evidence here, and what to skip. Precedence: channel > project > the recipe below.

**Ticketing Strategy bounds your recommendation** (`¶INV_TICKETING_STRATEGY_IN_HANDBOOK`). It lives in the project's **Inbox Handbook**; on a project predating the move it may still sit on the Project description, so fall back there. It arrives in its **own** block, separate from Directions — a different question: Directions steer what's worth *chasing*, this steers what's worth becoming *its own ticket* (Volume · Size · Substance). Your "ripe to graduate → which milestone" recommendation is a graduation-volume call, so weigh it against these bullets: when an item is well-formed but reads as a facet of something already tracked, recommend **fold into &lt;parent issue key&gt;** rather than a new ticket, and say which bullet drove that. "Not its own ticket" never means discard — it means fold or keep marinating. If the block is missing from your prompt, say so in the Boundary rather than assuming maximum ticketing.

**Stakeholders tell you who to ask** (optional; absent is normal). Facts about people, never authority — when a gap cannot be closed from the read-only sources, name the person who could close it in your **Boundary** rather than guessing or silently degrading to a weaker finding. Do **not** contact them yourself; you are read-only and report-only, and the orchestrator owns every outward message.

Honor them: when the Directions and the generic recipe below disagree, follow the Directions and record which line you followed. When the Directions ask for something you could not get, say so explicitly in the Boundary rather than silently substituting the default. No Directions present → the recipe below is the default, unchanged.

## Credential preflight — run this FIRST, and stop on a miss

```
engine env doctor --domain intake --tier triage
```

**A non-zero exit is a STOP.** Fix what it names and re-run; do not begin investigating. `--tier triage` evaluates only the rows a triage run needs, at the severity declared for triage — so a missing Slack token or an unauthenticated Notion MCP will not stop you, and a missing app login or read-only database credential will. Under `/intake` the wave has already gated at its Phase 0, and running it again costs a second and proves it for the run that is actually about to happen.

**Say the KEY, never a capability.** `¶INV_UNAVAILABLE_IS_A_CLAIM` forbids concluding a *capability* is gone from a diagnostic signal — a WARN on `AWS_PROFILE` once became *"no database"*, and the tunnel opened in twenty seconds. This gate asserts something different in kind: a named credential is absent, checked directly. So report *"`FINCH_AGENT_APP_PASSWORD` did not resolve — run `engine env setup --domain intake`"*, never *"the UI angle is unavailable"*. The first is a fact you verified; the second is an inference this protocol does not let you draw.

**Resolve secrets through the resolver, never a literal**: `engine env resolve <KEY> --show-value`. You never type or paste a credential, and one that does not resolve means an unprovisioned environment — say so plainly rather than asking anyone to send you one. The KEY NAMES come from the project's access directive below; the mechanism is the engine's.

**A `SEAM-INFLUENCED` run is not a verification.** Test seams (`ENV_MANIFEST`, `ENV_MCP_LIST_OUTPUT`, `ENV_STS_ARN`, …) were set, so the green says nothing about the real setup — re-run with them unset.

## Reaching the product — resolve this after the preflight

This skill names **no product**. Everything concrete — which hosts to open, how to sign in, how to reach a read-only database, which URL patterns build a deep link, which UI quirks will mislead you — comes from the project, in this order:

1.  **The project's triage-access directive** — **`.directives/INBOX_TRIAGE_ACCESS.md` at the repo root. READ IT BY PATH, as your first act, before you plan anything.** It is the authority on hosts, credentials, data access and app-driving mechanics. Finch's is the worked example.
    ⚠️ **`engine discover-directives` will NOT return it** — that command walks a fixed list of filenames and this is not one of them. Nothing auto-loads this file. **Reading it is your job, by name, and it is the reason this step exists.**
    **An absent access directive and an unread one look identical from the inside**, and the failure surfaces much later wearing a disguise: a capability that appears not to exist, or a plausible route that turns out to be the fallback. One run reached for a public database instance gated by an IP allowlist — whose failure mode is a silent timeout with no permission error — while holding the tunnel grant that needed no allowlist at all. It produced a confident, well-argued diagnosis of the wrong thing. **`cat` the path. If it is genuinely absent, say so in the Boundary rather than improvising a route.**
2.  **The Inbox Handbook's `## What triage will chase`** — the per-project recipe, which names the tools that identify a subject *in this project* and may override the app base.
3.  **The handoff prompt**, when a wave dispatched you and already resolved a shared endpoint.

**Absent all three, degrade rather than guess.** Work from whatever is reachable, and say plainly in the Boundary that the project publishes no access directive — that absence is a finding about the project, not a failure of the run. Never invent a hostname, a role name or a URL pattern.

## The recipe (do what's reachable; degrade gracefully when a source is missing)

**Branch by report shape first.** A single failure that needs a *cause* → start from the most detailed per-incident record (the workflow history, the specific failed row). A recurring "keeps happening" report that needs the *pattern* → start from an aggregate/histogram query (count the failures for rate, onset and breadth), then drill into whichever individual incidents are still recoverable — the aggregate is what reveals that a "recurring" report is often several different failures, not one bug.

1.  **Identify** — from the screenshots/description, pin the concrete subject: **which account/org**, and **which record** plus its **page URL**. Match on the names, identifiers and dates visible in the capture. The access directive names the instruments — typically a read-only database for turning a capture into ids, and an in-app selector for when you have a name but no id. Note every secondary identifier the capture shows; it is what makes the next lookup a substitution rather than a re-derivation.
2.  **Product analytics** (if the report is sourced from them — a session link or an event): pull the session plus the surrounding events for what the user actually did and saw.
3.  **Reproduce** — attempt a repro from the identified state; record steps + frequency (always / intermittent / couldn't). For data issues (e.g. "extraction failed 5×"), confirm the current stored state rather than a live UI repro.

    **Where the project provisions an agent account, you can open the customer's screens yourself, as yourself.** Triage used to stop at a link for someone else to click. Seeing the screen is often the finding, because a capture frequently fails to show the thing the reporter says it shows. **The mechanics are the access directive's** — credentials from the resolver rather than a literal, the sign-in traps, the wait conditions, how to point a session at an account, which navigation quirks produce a confidently-wrong capture, and where captures may be written. Read it before driving anything; every rule in it was earned on a run that went wrong without it.

    Two rules hold regardless of project, because both are about what your run leaves behind:

    *   **Captures never land in the repo.** A full-page screenshot of a customer's data, and an `innerText` dump of the same, are the same disclosure in two forms — and the second one greps. Write them to a temp directory, cite the path in the report, and attach anything that must persist to the ticket, which is access-controlled in a way a repository is not.
    *   **Every script this run writes is TypeScript. Never Python.** That covers the repro, an ad-hoc aggregation, a one-off parser — anything executable you leave behind or run to reach a finding. Write `.ts` and run it with `npx tsx <path>`.
        **Why, so the rule survives contact with a moment where Python looks easier** (and it will — it usually is, for the first ten minutes): the subject of every triage is a **TypeScript monorepo**, so a `.ts` script imports the project's own Drizzle schema, its clients and its env loading, and inherits the types that make a wrong column a compile error rather than a wrong answer. A Python script re-implements all of that against a hand-written idea of the schema, and **a repro that quietly drifts from the schema is worse than no repro** — it runs, it prints something, and the number is stale. Two further costs land on other people: the repo has **no Python toolchain**, so whoever inherits your repro cannot run it without setting one up; and a Python script needs its own connection and credential handling, which is a new place for a `data_ro` URL to end up in plaintext. Neither cost is visible to you at the moment you choose.
        **If you catch yourself reaching for Python because the TypeScript setup is friction, that friction is the finding** — say so in the Boundary. It means the repo makes its own data hard to query, which is worth more to the reader than the script was.
    *   **Attach the script you wrote; do NOT turn it into a test.** If you drove the app to reproduce something, save it to `builds/inbox-triage-<origin-id>[-<angle>]-repro.ts` and hand that path back — it is the cheapest possible handoff, because whoever takes the fix inherits a working reproduction instead of rebuilding one from your prose.
        **It uploads as its OWN attachment — it does NOT go inside the dossier.** The one-card rule (`§INV_CHANNEL_EVIDENCE_RIDES_THE_COMMENT`) governs **prose** evidence, where four attachments compete for one reader's attention. A script is not prose: it is picked up by a *different person for a different purpose*, and whoever takes the fix runs it. Fenced inside a 30 KB markdown dossier it has to be found, extracted, un-fenced and re-named before it can run, which destroys the exact property being claimed for it. **Under `--no-post` you upload nothing** — return the path; the caller uploads it (`§INV_TERMINAL_PRODUCER_POSTS`). But leave it as a repro. **A repro and a regression test answer different questions**: a repro asks *does this happen*, a test asks *does this still not happen*, and the second can only be written once the fix is known, which triage deliberately does not determine. A repro also hard-codes the real account and record it was about — committed as a test, that puts customer identifiers in the repo and breaks the moment that data legitimately changes. Say in the report that the script is a repro and what a test would need; that is the useful sentence, not a half-made test.

4.  **Relate** — search existing issues (a `list_issues`-style query on the account / symptom / component) for duplicates or the same root cause, **then OPEN each promising candidate through `§CMD_READ_RELATED_TICKET`** (the full issue + its comments + its state), never a bare issue fetch and never the search stub alone. A search call returns issue stubs and never a thread, so a Relate that stops there has read no prior triage, no retraction, no design constraint — the very things this step exists to find (see the bold note below, and `§INV_POINT_IN_TIME_CHECKS_EXPIRE` for why the read must be fresh at the moment you use it). List candidate issue keys with why-related, and say for each whether you opened it or only saw the stub. **When the reporter is a repeat filer in this channel, run Relate *first*** — search their own recent drops before investigating, so you don't re-derive facts they already wrote down 48 hours ago.

    🔴 **Relate lands BEFORE you draft the verdict, not merely before you post it.** The ordering is not politeness toward the record — it is what stops you writing a conclusion you will have to withdraw. Where a project has an active prior wave, the existing tickets **and their comment threads** *are* the literature review: a retraction comment can reframe a numeric picture you would otherwise spend an hour deriving, and a design comment can invert the operational advice your own evidence appears to support.

    **Measured twice in one wave.** One run found a wide, populated, unread table and reached for the obvious *"switch the read on"* — then found, in a comment on the ticket that owned the cutover, that column grain runs against the fact layer, so flipping it that day would lose more than it revealed. **Same evidence, opposite advice**; the only difference was whether Relate had landed first. A second run in the same wave reported that two of its own conclusions did not survive its sweep, and said so unprompted.

    **If you catch yourself drafting while the sweep is still open, stop.** The draft will anchor you, and this is the cheapest step in the protocol.

## Triage craft

The recipe is the steps; this is the judgment that makes them land. Every rule here was earned on a real run and applies to **every** project — the per-project handbook names the tools, this names the habits.

*   **Find the join key first.** Before crossing surfaces (screenshot → database → workflow store → tickets), find the one id that unifies them and confirm it *is* the same id across each. A single value often wears several names in different tables; pin it once and every later lookup is a substitution, not a re-derivation.
*   **Route, don't chase.** A signal often belongs to a different project than the one it landed in — a mis-read field belongs to the extraction team, not the app team; a classification miss belongs to the pipeline, not the screen. Naming the right home *is* triage; route it rather than triaging the wrong subject deep.
*   **Is it the bug you think it is?** Several failures look identical from the UI and need opposite fixes: a bad extraction vs an auto-apply clobbering a human-edited field; a genuine crash vs a product limit tripping a guard; a deterministic failure vs one that recovers on retry. Name which one before you write it up.
*   **Check your evidence's shelf life before you promise a cause.** Every source ages out — a workflow store's retention, an audit table's window. Learn the window before you rely on it; if the evidence is already gone, say so in the Boundary rather than reconstructing the past from what the record reads *today* (it may have been revised since).
*   **A negative result is only as strong as the search behind it.** Before reporting that something is absent — no such ticket, no such row, no such owner — say *what you searched*, not just what you didn't find. One id lookup that misses is not proof of absence; an enumeration under a transposed name has overturned "it doesn't exist" more than once. **This applies with full force to "not reproducible".** One run declared a prior measurement's predicate unrecoverable after a sixteen-query sweep; the predicate was documented in full in a comment on a ticket those sixteen queries never reached, and the claim was overturned on review. *"I could not find it"* and *"it does not exist"* are different sentences, and only the first is ever yours to write — **a negative with no stated search is a false negative waiting to be quoted.**
*   **Pin the provenance.** Record the model id / pipeline version behind the thing you're judging. Provenance that doesn't pin it blends generations, and a later model change then reads as a mystery regression instead of expected drift.
*   **Leave a friction note for the recipe.** At the end of a run, jot where the recipe helped and where it misled — the table that didn't hold the error, the step that was a dead end, the ordering wrong for this report's shape. That note is the raw material `/inbox-handbook capture` turns into a Keep/Add/Cut recipe delta; without it the lesson decays into anecdote.

## Output
0.  **Reproduce links (REQUIRED)** — every entity identified renders as a **clickable app URL**, so the reader can one-click to reproduce. These appear in BOTH the report (a "Links (reproduce)" section) AND the orchestrator's comment. Record every entity id so the links can be built. **The app base and the URL patterns come from the project's access directive**, and the Inbox Handbook may override the base for a project that runs somewhere else — some projects have no separate production. Prefer the most-specific deep link (the exact tab, document or sub-view the finding is about), never just the record root.
1.  **Triage report** → `builds/inbox-triage-<origin-id>.md`, following `assets/TEMPLATE_TRIAGE_REPORT.md` **exactly**: Verdict box · Entities (ids + links) · Signal · Findings — each tagged `[confirmed]` / `[inferred]` / `[needs-source]` · **Queries verbatim** (the exact read-only queries + key results — reproducible, never prose descriptions) · Scope · Related · Recommendation & **Boundary** (what triage could NOT determine → research) · **Steering read** (process-steering, NOT solutions — `¶INV_STEERING_NOT_SOLUTIONS`: a legitimacy/confidence read + still-open questions + concrete what-to-measure-next + 3–5 suggested board options `{key,label}`, all oriented by the project's Directions; the orchestrator polishes the options into the Decision Board's steering widgets — options to react to, never a manufactured answer). Record the **data source + timestamp** (a live primary vs a nightly replica — data moves, and which one you read changes what a number means). In the report, ticket keys render via `§FMT_TICKET_LINK` and any reference to a **specific comment** (the origin signal, a related/duplicate comment) via `§FMT_TICKET_COMMENT_LINK` deep-links — never a bare comment id.
2.  **1-line headline** — the finding in one line, for the orchestrator to post inline.
3.  **The repro script's path, if you wrote one** — named separately in what you return, never only mentioned in prose. A path buried in a paragraph is a path the caller does not upload.

**`<origin-id>`** = the stable id of what's being triaged — the **origin comment id** for a comment signal, or the **issue key** for a ticket/sub-issue. Use it verbatim in the filename so concurrent triages never collide.

This skill produces the **report** only. The **handoff prompt** (`builds/inbox-triage-handoff-<origin-id>.md`) is authored + saved by the **orchestrator** (it's the prompt it wrote to dispatch you) — it is your *input*, not your output. The orchestrator bundles **both** (report + handoff) into the single dossier it links from the comment, so the full chain (question → answer) is reproducible without the comment body having to narrate it.

**Post to the origin comment thread first**, then return the report path + posted comment URL + recommendation + headline.

**What you post is a comment written from `assets/TEMPLATE_TICKET_COMMENT.md` — not the report.** Write the report to `builds/` first, then *project its findings* into the comment shape: what's wrong · what the reporter got right and wrong · where (ids + deep links) · the evidence · how widespread · is it still happening · what to do · not established · a one-line `<sub>` footer. Then **upload the report and link it once** as a clickable `[📄 filename](assetUrl)` (`§INV_CHANNEL_EVIDENCE_RIDES_THE_COMMENT`) — naming a file in prose is not linking it, and the link must stand **alone in its own block, nothing after it on that line**, or the tracker renders it as plain text instead of a preview card.

**Sections of your report that must NOT survive into the comment**: the `**Angle**:` header · the whole `Steering read` (it is written for the orchestrator's Decision Board — `¶INV_STEERING_NOT_SOLUTIONS`) · the `[confirmed]`/`[inferred]`/`[needs-source]` basis tags (in a comment the cited source *is* the basis) · `Queries (verbatim)` (they live in the attached report) · and any framing of `Boundary` as a limit of *the investigation* — rewrite each open item as a property of **the defect**, with the cheapest thing that would settle it.

If you are one angle of a triangulation you are `--no-post` and none of this applies to you — return your report and let the wrapper publish.

## Data access (read-only)

**Prefer the path that guarantees read-only by GRANT, not by a flag.** A role with no write grant cannot be talked out of it; a session setting can be flipped, by you or by anything that shares your connection.

The concrete path — the host, the role, the secret, the tunnel and its gotchas — is the **project's access directive**. What binds regardless of project:

*   **Never connect as a read-write role.** If ever forced to, set the session read-only *and* open an explicitly read-only transaction — but treat that as a guardrail, not a guarantee.
*   **When a wave dispatched you, it has already opened the shared connection.** Use the endpoint the handoff prompt names; do not open your own. A second tunnel usually fails to bind and costs the whole wave the port.
*   **Record the source and the timestamp on every number you report.** A live primary and a nightly replica disagree, and a reader cannot tell which you read unless you say.

## Constraints
*   **¶INV_COMMENT_REPORTS_THE_SUBJECT_NOT_THE_METHOD**: A comment posted to a ticket is addressed to **the person who owns the bug**, and reports the bug. The investigation record is a *different artifact for a different reader* — it is attached, never pasted. Method-talk gets one `<sub>` line at the bottom naming the method, the provenance and the date; above that line, every sentence's subject is the defect. **Detail is not the enemy** — a long, dense, evidence-heavy comment is often the right one; a comment that spends its length on how the answer was reached is not. **Reason, measured**: a triangulated run on [FIN-3940](https://linear.app/finchclaims/issue/FIN-3940) published a comment roughly half of which was about the investigation — which sub-run checked which commit, whether their agreement was genuine, a critique of the orchestrator's own briefing, and that briefing attached for audit. The findings under it were correct and well-evidenced; the reader was a product owner who wanted to know whether a money column was still wrong. **The protocol had required it**, so the fix is here in the protocol rather than in anyone's discipline. Practical test: read your draft as someone who has never heard of these skills. If a paragraph teaches them about triage instead of about the defect, it belongs in the report.
*   **Read-only, everywhere.** Database access is via a role that is SELECT-only *by grant*. Never mutate product data. Never create/edit/close tickets. Never call the tracker's save-comment/save-issue tools — *except* the single permitted comment when running `--post`.
*   **Evidence over assertion.** Every identification cites the record/query that supports it.
*   **Bounded.** This is triage, not a deep dive — if closing a gap needs real research, say so in the recommendation (→ `Needs research`) rather than doing it here.
