Layout exemplars  ·  for /prove evidence pages

Twenty-five layouts to adapt — the shapes a proof page can take.

The static block catalog (PROOF_BLOCKS.html) answers which part; this gallery answers what shape. Every pattern below is real, rendered markup on the .mod grammar — open the source, lift the snippet, swap the content. These are inspiration to remix, not a closed set: the pattern is the geometry plus the when-to-use, never any final CSS. Each carries its name, when to reach for it, and how to remix it in the words of the exemplar it came from.

Sample content Every figure is illustrative — it demonstrates the layout, not a live finding. Zero-JS · CSP-safe · theme-aware · system fonts · inline SVG only.

§ 03

Data-as-visual

Five hand-authored inline-SVG charts. No library, no CDN, no script for the marks: length, position and hue carry the numbers, and each states its scale in a legend. — 5 patterns.

03.1

Confidence band

¶

A single estimate on a bounded scale, with its uncertainty interval shaded and the point estimate marked. Reads at a glance: where the value sits, and how sure we are.

03.2

Coverage heatmap

¶

A grid of rows × checks where each cell is tinted by a real value and prints the number. The eye finds the cold column and the weak row before reading a single digit.

03.3

Distribution / histogram

¶

Warm-toned bars over binned buckets, with a labeled x-axis and a marked median. Shows the shape of the data — the long tail the average hides.

03.4

Bars-in-table

¶

A real table where one column carries an inline mini-bar beside the number. You get the exact digits and the visual ranking in the same row — no separate chart to cross-reference.

03.5

Gauge / meter row

¶

A row of small radial gauges, each a single KPI against its threshold — green when it clears the target, amber when it's close, red when it fails. Built for a dashboard header strip.

§ 04

Annotated evidence

Four ways to point at the part of an exhibit that carries the proof. The shared discipline: every cross-reference is anchored by a shared number or by in-viewBox geometry, never by a page coordinate that reflow can break. — 4 patterns.

04.1

Callout-pins overlay

¶

Numbered pins → keyed list

PrincipleDrop numbered pins on the regions that matter, then decode each below in a keyed list. Pins and list share their numbers by construction (CSS counters, one per list) and are two-way linked: click a pin to highlight its note, click a note badge to jump back to its pin. Percentage-positioned pins ride the image on any width.

When to useA busy exhibit where several separate spots each need a sentence — a full screen, a dense form, a multi-region PDF page.

How to remixSwap the placeholder <svg> for your real <img src="data:…"> render (keep it inside .pinstage). Move each .pin by editing its left/top percentages — they track the image on reflow. Add or drop a pin + its <li> together; the counters renumber both automatically.

04.2

Leader-line margin annotations

¶

In-SVG leaders → margin notes, tied by number

PrincipleAnnotations sit in the margin, connected to the exhibit by leader lines. The durable lesson from the composition page: fixed-coordinate leader lines break on reflow. So the lines are drawn inside the exhibit SVG (viewBox coordinates — they scale with the image, never desync), ending at numbered tabs at the edge. The margin note carries the same number badge — that badge is the real anchor. Stack the layout and the notes still map by number; nothing dangles.

04.3

Highlighted-region crop + zoom

¶

Tinted region of interest → magnified detail call

PrincipleShow the whole exhibit for context, tint the one region that carries the proof, then re-draw that region enlarged as a detail call beside it — the way a field guide crops a wing. The tint is a keyed colour (see legend); the detail inset repeats the exact region so the reader can verify the small print without squinting.

05.3

Story arc — expected, discovered, therefore

¶
NameThe Three-Beat Feature WhenThe finding has a twist — what you expected wasn't what you found, and that reframes the fix. Give it magazine drama: a setup, a turning point, a resolution. RemixThree .beat blocks — setup, turn, resolve. The turn is tinted --defect, the resolve --seal. Lean on the oversized numeral, the eyebrow lead, and one pull-line per beat; keep prose at reading measure.
Setup
1
We expected

A handful of magnet claims — a known shape, a known detector.

The artifact-axis detector had cleaned magnet pollution before: a few high-fanout claims accreting attachments they shouldn't. We assumed the stranger-contact report was one more of those, and reached for the same tool.

"Run the magnet detector, detach the outliers, close the ticket. We'd done it twice before."
Turning point
2
But we discovered

The detector found nothing — yet the pollution was everywhere.

The artifact axis flagged zero claims. But a lineage query found 214 auto-approved thread-sibling links on perfectly ordinary claims — no fanout, no magnet shape. The pollution wasn't accreting on outliers; it was seeping along email threads, one reply at a time.

The real culprit was upstream: a propagation_eligibility that had been stamped eligible and then never re-evaluated after a Layer-4 verification downgrade.

"The tool that had worked twice was blind to the actual mechanism. We'd been treating the symptom's shape, not its cause."
Resolution
3
Therefore

A new axis, a recompute, and a guard that can't drift.

We added a lineage axis to the unwind tool, recomputed eligibility from each row's own rationale codes, and wired a policy-version guard so a downgraded link can never keep a stale verdict. The repair detached 211 of 214 links; the guard stops the next one at the source.

214 links found  ·  211 detached  ·  3 held for review

06.4

Trusted-vs-checked provenance ribbon

¶

PatternProvenance Ribbon. A compact two-state visual language: a coloured left spine marks each assertion's provenance so the reader always knows, at a glance, what rests on the upstream finding versus what was verified here. When to use: flowing prose or a mixed narrative where claims of both kinds interleave. How to remix: the ribbon is the whole vocabulary — solid accent spine = checked-here, hatched spine = trusted-upstream. Keep the legend adjacent; never introduce a third state without adding it to the legend.

the two states
checked-hereRe-verified for this page. A solid accent spine. The assertion was re-run, re-rendered, or re-read against source during the presentation pass.
trusted-upstreamCarried from the finding. A hatched spine. Taken as given from the resolved investigation; /prove does not re-litigate it.
why it matters
▮A reader must never mistake a re-check for a repetition. The spine is a promise: solid means we looked again; hatched means we did not, and say so.
